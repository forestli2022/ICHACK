Neurodivergence Research Assistant

Making academic research accessible to educators, clinicians, caregivers, and advocacy organizations.

Problem

Research on neurodivergence (ADHD, autism, dyslexia, etc.) is vast, fragmented, and written primarily for academics.

Educators, charities, and support organizations often lack the time, training, or access needed to extract actionable insights from peer-reviewed literature.

Solution

This tool acts as a research interpreter. Users can ask practical questions in plain language and receive evidence-based answers grounded in academic sources.

Each response is transparent, citing the papers used and clearly separating evidence from interpretation.

Ask a Research Question

Summary Answer

Research suggests that structured routines, short task segments, and multimodal instruction significantly improve attention and task completion for children with ADHD.

Supporting Evidence

Smith et al. (2019)
Found that breaking tasks into 10–15 minute intervals improved sustained attention in primary school classrooms.
Jones & Patel (2021)
Demonstrated that visual schedules reduced cognitive load and anxiety, indirectly improving focus.

Why This Matters

  • Bridges the gap between academia and real-world practice
  • Supports inclusive education and accessibility
  • Reduces misinformation and oversimplification
  • Empowers non-academic stakeholders with evidence

Technical Approach

  • Curated academic paper dataset (pre-indexed)
  • Embedding-based semantic search (RAG)
  • Transparent citation of source material
  • Expandable knowledge base using trusted APIs

Feasibility & Scalability

The prototype uses a limited set of papers for speed and reliability. The same architecture can scale to thousands of publications across different neurodivergent conditions and languages.
